# Install Quoth on Android

This guide takes you from installing Quoth to your first dictation. It takes about 5 minutes, plus waiting while the speech models download.

## Before you start

Quoth needs:

- **Android 10 or later on a 64-bit phone.** Most phones from 2019 on qualify. To check your version: **Settings → About phone → Android version**.
- **About 150 MB of free space** for the speech model, or **about 1.6 GB** with the optional clean-up model (which removes ums and fixes punctuation).
- **Wi-Fi** for the clean-up model's download is a good idea: it's 1.4 GB.

## Step 1: Install

Open Quoth's page on Google Play and tap **Install**. While Quoth is in testing, use the link you were sent: it opens a page where you tap **Become a tester**, then **download it on Google Play**.

## Step 2: Set it up

Open **Quoth** from your home screen. The first screen lists the setup steps; each turns into a green tick when it's done.

1. **Allow the microphone.** Tap **Allow**, then **While using the app**. Quoth only listens while you've tapped its microphone button.
2. **Download the speech model** (148 MB). Tap **Download** and wait for the tick.
3. **Download the clean-up model** (1.4 GB, optional). Tap **Download**. You can use Quoth while it downloads; the clean-up starts working once it's done.
4. **Turn on the Quoth keyboard.** Tap **Open settings**, then switch on **Quoth voice keyboard**. Android shows a warning that a keyboard may collect everything you type. That warning appears for every keyboard. Quoth reads only a little text around the cursor while you dictate, and nothing leaves your phone ([Privacy](privacy-android.md)). Tap **OK**, then go back to Quoth.
5. **Switch to Quoth.** Tap **Choose keyboard** and pick **Quoth voice keyboard**.

When everything is done, the steps fold away and you see **✓ Quoth is ready**.

## Step 3: Your first dictation

1. Tap the box under **Try it here**. The Quoth keyboard appears, with a big green microphone.
2. Tap the microphone and say something, like *"Hi Sam, um, see you at three"*.
3. Tap the microphone again. After a second or two, the text appears: *"Hi Sam, see you at 3."*

## Using Quoth in other apps

Quoth works next to your usual keyboard:

- **To dictate:** in any app, tap the **keyboard button** (a small keyboard icon at the bottom right of the screen, or a globe key on your keyboard) and pick Quoth. Then tap the microphone, speak, and tap again.
- **To type again:** tap **⌨** on the Quoth keyboard. It switches straight back to your usual keyboard.
- **⌫** deletes and **⏎** sends or starts a new line, as on any keyboard.

Quoth switches itself off in password boxes.

## Make it yours

From Quoth's home screen:

- **Dictionary:** add names and words Quoth should spell your way. When you correct a name right after dictating, Quoth learns it (or use **Fix** in History).
- **Snippets:** say a short phrase, like "my address", and Quoth types the full text.
- **Style:** choose how casual Quoth writes in chats, work apps and email.
- **Settings:** the clean-up level, your languages, and how long History is kept.

## If something doesn't work

- **"Open the Quoth app to allow the microphone"** on the keyboard: open Quoth and tap **Allow** in step 1. If you denied it before, go to **Settings → Apps → Quoth → Permissions → Microphone** and choose **Allow only while using the app**.
- **The first dictation after restarting the phone says "Getting ready…"** while the models load. That takes a few seconds, once.
- **The text appears slowly:** the clean-up model needs a fairly recent phone. On an older phone, choose **None** in **Settings → Clean-up model**; dictation then takes only the speech model's couple of seconds.
- **A download stopped:** tap **Try again**. It continues where it left off.
